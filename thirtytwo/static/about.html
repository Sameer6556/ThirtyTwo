Thirty Two

About

Thirty Two is named after the 32 adult teeth. It marks four kinds of findings on a panoramic dental X-ray and gives each one the number of its tooth.

What it finds

  • Caries C

    Decay in a tooth.

  • Deep caries DC

    Decay that has reached deep into the tooth, close to the pulp.

  • Periapical lesion PL

    A dark area around the tip of a root, usually a sign of infection.

  • Impacted tooth IT

    A tooth that has not grown into its place, often a wisdom tooth.

Tooth numbers

Teeth are numbered the FDI way. The first digit is the quarter of the mouth, the second counts from the front tooth (1) to the wisdom tooth (8). The chart is drawn the way a panoramic X-ray shows the mouth, so the patient's right side is on the left.

Upper teeth are on the top row, lower teeth on the bottom row. Tooth 36, outlined, is the first molar of the lower jaw on the patient's left.

What it is for

Thirty Two is a research prototype. It shows, on any panoramic X-ray, what a system trained on a public set of X-rays can find and where it falls short. It is for research and teaching, not for patient care: it is not a medical device, and it should not be used to diagnose or treat anyone.

Privacy

Your X-ray is processed in memory and never stored. Nothing about it is written to disk or to the logs, and the page loads nothing from other websites.

Data and licence

Thirty Two learned from DENTEX, a public set of panoramic X-rays from three hospitals, released for the DENTEX challenge at MICCAI 2023. Its models were trained on 635 X-rays labelled by experts, its settings were picked on 70 others, and some models were trained further on 1,961 X-rays without diagnosis labels.

DENTEX is released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so Thirty Two is for research and teaching only and may not be used commercially. The two sample X-rays are unmodified files from the DENTEX validation set and stay under that licence (notice).
