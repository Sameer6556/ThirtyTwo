How it works

Thirty Two looks at an X-ray in two ways at once: tooth by tooth, and as a whole. Then it combines the answers into one set of boxes, each with the number of its tooth.

  1. 1

    Find the teeth

    A tooth detector finds and numbers every tooth.

    One class per FDI tooth number, 11 to 48. These numbers are the ones on every finding.

  2. 2

    Look closely

    A classifier checks each tooth up close.

    Each tooth is cut out with extra room at the root, and teeth on one side are mirrored so that both sides of the jaw look alike. Two classifiers do this: the original and a self-trained one.

  3. 3

    Scan the whole X-ray

    Detectors scan the whole X-ray.

    Seven detectors look for the four findings anywhere on the image: three plain ones and four self-trained ones, at up to 1,536 pixels across.

  4. 4

    Vote

    Nine models vote, and boxes that several models agree on score higher.

    When a classifier marks a tooth, its finding takes the detectors' box for that tooth.

Some of the models were trained further on 1,961 extra X-rays that the system labelled itself.
